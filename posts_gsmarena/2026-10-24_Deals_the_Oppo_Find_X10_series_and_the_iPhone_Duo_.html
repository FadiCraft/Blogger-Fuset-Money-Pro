This week saw two major launches – the Oppo Find X10 series went global and the Apple iPhone Duo started shipping. Let’s have a look at the Oppo first.

The Oppo Find X10 Pro Max is a beast of a phone – in our review, we call it “an Ultra in all but name”. The triple 200MP camera is impressive, the battery life is exceptional, the chipset is powerful and the display is beautiful. What more can you ask for? Well, a lower price would be nice.

Right now, Amazon has it with a €100 discount and a choice of one freebie – you can pick between the Oppo Pad 5 with a Matte Display (8/128GB, Wi-Fi, €400), the Oppo Watch X3 (€310) and the Oppo Hasselblad Teleconverter Kit (€550) that goes with the phone. The kit includes a 3.3x/70mm lens (this stacks with the phone’s own magnification for an effective 10x/230mm focal length), the necessary case, a grip and a lanyard. Check out this photo comparison to see how much of a difference the kit makes (and for an overview of some issues it brings).

The Oppo Find X10 also has a €100 discount and the same choice of one of three freebies. This phone is smaller, 6.59” vs. 6.78” display, and uses the Dimensity 9600M chipset (which is based on 9500-level hardware). The Pro Max uses the powerful new Dimensity 9600 Pro instead. The camera hardware on the vanilla model, while still impressive, is no match for the Pro Max either. It has a 200MP main (1/1.4” vs. 1/1.3” sensor size), a 200MP telephoto (both 1/1.56, but 65mm vs. 70mm) and a 50MP ultra-wide (vs. 200MP).

Note that the European Find X10 phones have 7,150mAh batteries instead of 8,000mAh like the units sold elsewhere.

Its cameras are not like the other Ultras, but they do include a 200MP main (with a relatively small 1/1.56” sensor), a 50MP 5x/114mm periscope and a 50MP ultra-wide. Now, let’s look at the competition. The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 is the only other wide foldable in Europe – the Xiaomi 18 Fold is still pending. It currently has a massive discount, which brings the starting price down to €1,400 – this is €900 less than the Duo. The discount on the 512GB variant is much smaller, though.

The Galaxy is thinner (9.7mm vs. 11.3mm) and much lighter (201g vs. 254g) while offering a similar cover display (5.5” vs. 5.4”) and inner display (both 7.6” with similar aspect ratios). They have nearly identical battery capacities too (4,800mAh vs. 4,883mAh), so endurance will depend on chipset efficiency – it’s a Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for the Galaxy and an Apple A20 Pro for the Duo. Both phones lack dedicated telephoto cameras, offering only dual 50MP and dual 48MP modules, respectively.

Samsung also has a larger book-style foldable, the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra. Despite having a larger 6.5” cover display (a tall and narrow 21:9 panel) and an 8.0” inner display, the Ultra remains thin and light – 8.9mm and 215g (that is 39g less than the Duo). This one packs a 200MP 1/1.3” sensor in the main camera and does have a telephoto lens, even if it’s only a 10MP 3x/69mm module.
