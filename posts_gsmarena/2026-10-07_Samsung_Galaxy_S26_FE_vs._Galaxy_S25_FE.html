The Fan Edition of the latest Galaxy S series is out, but before you pull the trigger on the Samsung Galaxy S26 FE, why not consider the Samsung Galaxy S25 FE instead. It gets you a lot of the way there at a more reasonable starting price, which is exactly the FE series spirit. After all, the memory chip prices forced Samsung to launch the S26 FE at a higher price point and without changing almost anything in terms of hardware too.

The newer Galaxy S26 FE is pretty close to last year's Galaxy S25 FE, except for the chipset, of course. And one would wonder, what are you getting for the extra €50/$100? Let's find out!

The newer Galaxy S26 FE is virtually identical to the Galaxy S25 FE when it comes to dimensions, weight and design. The newer Galaxy is negligibly wider and taller, and adds 3 grams to the overall weight. Not the kind of difference you will feel in the real world.

There's also no change in the build materials - both devices have Gorilla Glass Victus+ glass sheets on the front and back, while the side frame is "Enhanced Armor Aluminum," which is Samsung's own aluminum alloy used for its flagship phones.

What's new here is the colors. The new S26 FE comes in three colors - Blueberry, Graphite and Pistachio. The older Galaxy had four color options - Icyblue, Jetblack, Navy and White. We doubt that colors will be the deciding factor, but it's perhaps still important to note.

The two Galaxy Fan Editions share the same 6.7-inch OLED display with the same feature set. Our brightness tests confirm our suspicion that Samsung has ported the older screen directly to the new S26 FE.

Battery life

Here's where it gets interesting, though. Despite the lack of upgrades in the battery department, the newer Galaxy S26 FE turns out to be more efficient than its predecessor. Since the display and battery remain unchanged, the jump in endurance must be due to the newer Exynos 2500 SoC.

The Galaxy S26 FE's higher Active Use Score is solely thanks to the strong uptick in web browsing and video streaming.

The two handsets support up to 45W fast wired charging over Power Delivery with the correct PPS profile charger, but Samsung promises a marginal update in the charging curve, allowing slightly faster charging than before. Our tests corroborate those claims, to some extent.

The Galaxy S26 FE is 4 percentage points faster to the 15 minute checkpoint and 6 percentage points faster at the 30-minute mark, but only 2 minutes faster to the finish line at 100% charge. In other words - no meaningful change in the charging speed.

Speaker test

Unsurprisingly, Samsung didn't swap out the speakers for newer ones. The Galaxy S26 FE seemingly uses the same stereo loudspeaker setup as its predecessor with near-identical tuning, too. The only marginal difference is loudness, as the older model is a tad louder.

Performance

The Galaxy S26 FE runs on Samsung's last year's flagship Exynos 2500 chipset, while the Galaxy S25 FE gets the Exynos 2400 from the year 2024. The newer SoC is touted as more powerful and more energy efficient, which our endurance and performance tests confirm.

In the standard benchmark tests, the Exynos 2500 inside the S26 FE outperforms the Exynos 2400-powered S25 FE by about 16% in the CPU runs and by almost 25% in the GPU-focused 3DMark Wild Life test. The S26 FE also showed better sustained performance in our regular CPU and GPU stress tests.

Even though the performance difference is measurable, it may go unnoticed if you are not really into gaming.

Both phones ship with 7 years of major OS upgrades, but since the Galaxy S25 FE launched last year, its support will end a year earlier. Also, Samsung removed the wired DeX support on the newer model and no longer has DisplayPort output. This is quite the bummer for people used to wired DeX. Wireless DeX is here to stay, at least.

Camera comparison

The Galaxy S26 FE comes with the same camera system as last year's model - not just in terms of sensors and resolutions, but the exact same hardware (which was barely an upgrade over the S24 FE before it).

The main camera uses the in-house GN3 sensor that has a 50MP resolution and a 1/1.56" optical format. The telephoto is based on a relatively tiny 1/4.4" sensor with just 8MP resolution, though it will get you 12MP images. The ultrawide uses a fixed-focus lens, as does the selfie camera, and both feature 12MP sensors.

The camera samples (both photo and video) have been taken on separate occasions with the two phones.

The new model just might be a little bit more restrained with the sharpening and can possibly render the detail a little finer, but it's not a huge difference. Both have lively color output and no problems with dynamic range.

Low light

A lot may have changed in the lighting of some of our low-light scenes, but little has changed in the way the Galaxy FE behaves in the dark - the S26 FE and the S25 FE capture similar levels of detail, tend to leave quite a bit of grain the images, and are not particularly keen on using Night mode if left to their own devices. The S25 FE does tend to be a bit more contrasty in its output, next to a pretty washed out S26 FE, so in a way the old model can be seen as a little better.



Low-light comparison, main camera (1x): Galaxy S26 FE • Galaxy S25 FE

Night mode cleans up the noise and dials up the sharpening on both phones, and the difference is more pronounced on the S26 FE. We wouldn't say it makes the new model take the lead though. It's probably a semi-curious observation that we weren't able to get the phone to apply night mode processing on the fourth scene - that's consistency for you right there.

Verdict

As you can see, the updates on the Galaxy S26 FE are far and few between, but the chipset does make a difference. Not only is it faster, it also delivers an upgrade is the battery endurance, as the S26 FE has a particularly strong showing in our web browsing and video playback tests.

But the rest remains largely unchanged. And the exclusion of wired DeX and DisplayPort video out support don't help build a stronger case for the S26 FE.

So is the newer model worth it? Well, the answer in Europe is most probably "yes" because the price gap isn't big to begin with. For about €50 more, you get the newer model, unless the missing wired DeX support is a deal-breaker to you. On the other hand, the $100 gap in the US arguably makes the S25 FE the better deal.




    Get the Samsung Galaxy S26 FE for:

  • The year longer OS support.
  • The slightly more powerful chipset.

    Get the Samsung Galaxy S25 FE for:

  • The roughly the same user experience at a lower price.
  • The wired DeX support and DisplayPort video out.
