Now that Samsung has a new form factor in the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8, it is only natural for at least some prospective buyers to be weighing it against the more established foldable design in the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra. After all, the difference in price between the two isn't huge, so the decision has to be based on other considerations.

So, let's look into some of the finer points in this comparison together. Perhaps we might just help you figure out which is the best Fold for you.

Let's kick things off with the most obvious and the most important of comparisons – the proportions. The Z Fold8 Ultra is clearly the bigger phone. And even though the Z Fold8 Ultra is technically thinner, it still feels more substantial.

Granted, this is going to be subjective, but we think that the new Z Fold8 is both more pocket-friendly and easier to handle. Not to mention more versatile.

Here's how the two foldables stack up when it comes to displays and their advertised aspect ratios (the actual ones are slightly different, but these are definitely close enough).

Cover and main screen comparison: Galaxy Z Fold8 vs. Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

Cover display comparison

Let's start with the cover display first. We can do some quick napkin math to help us out. The Z Fold8 Ultra cover display has an area of about 98.7 cm², while the Z Fold8 has around 88.2 cm² to work with. Not drastically different in absolute terms (The Ultra cover has 11.9% more total area), but definitely very different in proportions. The Fold8 cover is about 14.9% physically wider in portrait. The Ultra cover screen is about 28.6% taller.

Let's go on with our handy math then. Say you want to watch 16:9 video content, which is the most prevalent aspect out there. The Fold8's cover display will actually provide about a 4.2% bigger picture with considerably smaller black lines. 4:3 video is even more dramatic of a win for the Fold8 cover display, which offers about a 32% larger picture. The only real win in this department for the Z Fold8 Ultra is with 21:9 cinematic video in particular, which is about 65% larger on the Ultra's cover display.

Next, let's look at social media. Most of it is designed around tall aspect ratios, so you will generally see more posts on the Z Fold8 Ultra's cover display. While neither display is perfect for 9:16 vertical video, the Ultra is a lot closer. However, there is a tradeoff here and as far as individual posts go, especially photos, these will generally look bigger on the Z Fold8's cover display.

General web browsing is an interesting comparison. With mobile-formatted webpages, the taller Ultra's cover screen is able to fit more content. However, desktop-style pages definitely feel more at home on the Z Fold8's wider cover display. Text wraps into fewer lines. Wide horizontal spreadsheets and similar table layouts just plain look better.

And speaking of browsing the web, messengers just unquestionably feel better on the Z Fold8's wider cover display. Typing on there is a real pleasure too.

The Fold8 cover's roughly 1.58:1 portrait shape is close to the proportions of many paperback and e-book formats. It feels refreshingly nice for reading. And things like comics and manga are usually in a 3:2 aspect, which also feels a lot better on the Z Fold8's cover display.

Finally, gaming on the cover displays is an interesting topic. In modern titles with proper widescreen support, the Z Fold8 Ultra takes the cake. But for compatibility, emulation, 16:9 games, and potentially getting physically larger touch controls, the Z Fold8's cover is better overall.

Main display comparison

Ok, time for the main display comparison. The Fold8's main panel has a total area of around 177.8 cm², while the Z Fold8 Ultra's main display covers around 204.4 cm². That's a 15% total difference in favor of the Ultra. Again, it's all about the sort of content you intend to view here. The Ultra's main display is about 17.8% wider in portrait. The Fold8's main display is actually about 2.4% taller in portrait.

Let's look at the video playback area first, since that is what really surprises most. The Ultra main display has 15% more total area, but it is almost square. Its landscape width is slightly smaller than the regular Fold8's. As a result, video windows in pretty much every popular aspect are about 5% larger on the Z Fold8 (around 4% for 4:3).

Better still, the Z Fold8 uses much more of its display with smaller black bars: about 75% display coverage in 16:9 compared to about 62% on the Ultra. A whopping 99% and above for 4:3 content, compared to 83% on the Ultra and about 57% coverage for 21:9 cinematic, compared to 48% on the Ultra. In plain terms, less of the screen is wasted and watching videos feels more natural without the wide black bars.

On to social media. This is one area where neither phone is amazing on its main display. The regular Fold8's 4:3 format is already unusually wide for portrait social apps, but the Ultra is even squarer. That can lead to things like very wide posts, large empty side margins and more unused space around vertical photos and videos. The Fold8's narrower portrait width makes Instagram, Facebook and X feel somewhat closer to conventional phone applications. However, the Ultra quickly gets a massive advantage in any app that has a proper tablet interface.

General web browsing and productivity tasks go to the Ultra's main display. In portrait orientation, the Ultra is around 18% wider. Websites can use that to show wider article columns, more complete desktop layouts, larger tables and charts, better two-column or dual-pane layouts and generally a more comfortable split-screen browsing experience.

The Fold8's 4:3 shape is almost ideal for displaying two conventional book pages side by side. A pair of approximately 3:2 portrait pages forms a 4:3 spread, matching the screen closely. It is also better for a single page because it has a more page-like portrait shape.

The Ultra provides more total surface for content, but its near-square proportions result in excess width for a single page and more empty space around standard pages. For things like comics and manga, surprisingly again, despite its smaller total area, the Z Fold8's main display provides a roughly 5% larger 3:2 image. However, for specific things like panel-by-panel viewing, zooming into artwork or keeping controls on screen beside the content, the Ultra is better.

For gaming, the Ultra ultimately has 15% more screen real estate. Games that adapt correctly to arbitrary aspect ratios will generally feel more expansive on the Ultra. This is especially relevant for strategy games, card games, board games, racing or action games that expand the viewport, games with maps, inventories or complex interfaces and emulation with on-screen controls around the image.

The Z Fold8's main display still gets a few gaming wins, albeit very specific ones. Things like fixed-aspect Android games will likely be 4 to 5% larger. Also, 4:3 retro gaming emulation will arguably look better.

Closing out this already bloated size comparison section, we can sum up that the regular Fold8 is the content-consumption specialist. Both of its screens are shaped more efficiently for conventional video, books, comics and fixed-aspect gaming.

The Fold8 Ultra, on the other hand, is the productivity and vertical-content specialist. Its cover is excellent for scrolling social feeds, long mobile web articles and ultrawide video, while its nearly square inner screen gives substantially more workspace for websites, spreadsheets, multitasking and games that can genuinely use the full panel.

We mostly covered the most important screen usability bits in the previous section already. Tech-wise, all four panels across both devices are actually very similar. All of them are LTPO panels capable of refresh rates up to 120Hz, and all of them peak at somewhere around 3,000 nits of brightness.

Despite what look like pretty different resolution figures at first glance, the two cover panels actually come very close in terms of actual pixel density. They are basically equally sharp, so to say.

The one real difference worth mentioning is that this year Samsung upgraded the main display resolution of its traditional form factor foldable. So, with 2256 x 2504 pixels on an 8-inch diagonal, the Z Fold8 Ultra has a pixel density of around 422 ppi, up from around 368 ppi on the Z Fold7. All the while, the Z Fold8 is rocking 1828 x 2448 pixels on a 7.6-inch diagonal, which works out to around 403 ppi. Honestly, the sharpness difference between the Z Fold8 and the Z Fold8 Ultra is not really noticeable with a naked eye.

Regardless of which phone you choose, you will get Samsung's excellent dynamic refresh rate control, superb HDR support and all that goodness.

The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra packs a 5,000 mAh battery – the largest in a Samsung foldable to date. This was at least partially achieved through the use of modern Si/C battery chemistry – another first for a Samsung device.

The Galaxy Z Fold8 is also using Si/C tech, but it has a slightly smaller 4,800 mAh battery.

Both the Z Fold8 and Z Fold8 Ultra offer pretty good battery life for a modern foldable. Despite its smaller battery, the Z Fold8 offers a bit better battery life, when using their main displays. If you prioritize working on the cover display more, then the Z Fold8 generally has a more convincing lead in the battery life department.

We get 45W charging on both Z Fold8's. This is achieved through what Samsung brands Super Fast Charging 2.0. It is based on USB Power Delivery (USB-PD) with PPS on top. In its current iteration, the protocol does not require a 5A cable to function properly. It works just fine with a 3A cable, as the one provided in the box, so you don't need a fancy charger to get the fastest possible charging speed.

It is worth remembering that the two phones do have slightly different battery capacities. Hence, it comes as no surprise that Samsung claims the Z Fold8 Ultra can charge up to 67% in thirty minutes, while the Z Fold8 can get about 63% charge in the same time.

In our testing, we actually managed to consistently outperform both of these figures, which is great. Ultimately, the Z Fold8 Ultra does charge a bit faster out of the two, but honestly it's not much of a difference in practical terms.

Speaker test

As mentioned, the speaker setup on the Z Fold8 looks a lot like that on the Z Fold8 Ultra. However, it's likely that Samsung had even less room for proper resonance chambers on the inside. Hence, it comes as no surprise that the Z Fold8 is a tad quieter than the Z Fold7, which was already a downgrade compared to the Z Fold6 and even that one only managed a "Good" rating in our loudness test. Well, unfortunately, the Z Fold8 just misses the cutoff for "Good" territory and falls into "Average". To be clear, it is not terrible, but don't expect room-filling sound.

Surprisingly, the Z Fold8 Ultra seems to be as quiet as the Z Fold8. A tad worse even, though the two tests are basically identical and within the margin of error of each other. So neither phone is really a winner in the speaker category.

While technically the Z Fold8 Ultra is the "pinnacle" flagship in the current lineup, Samsung decided not to treat the Z Fold8 Ultra as a second-class citizen when it comes to performance. At least on paper, that is.

Both phones are equipped with Qualcomm's still top-dog Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5. Better yet, it is the higher-clocked "for Galaxy" variant. Granted, this chip is a few months old at this point and due for a replacement, but that's just in the nature of foldable development and release cycles.

While there are slight differences in the memory configurations available for the two phones, the two are basically on par. Most users will end up with 12GB of LPDDR5X RAM. However, you can get 16GB of RAM if you really want to splurge. Storage starts at 256GB on both and goes up to 1TB. Non-expandable on both, and as far as we can tell, both use the same fast UFS 4.X chips.

Camera comparison

There's a relatively significant difference on paper between the two camera systems, with the Ultra obviously packing higher-tier hardware. That means a larger-sensor, higher-resolution main camera (1/1.3" 200MP vs. 1/1.56" 50MP) and an extra telephoto, though the extra 1/3.94" 10MP 3x camera is hardly cutting-edge. The ultrawides are the same between the two models, as are the two in-display selfie cameras.

So from the onset, logic would dictate that we should be expecting better zooming capabilities from the Ultra, and potentially better low-light performance from its main camera, while the rest should be more or less identical.

Video quality

The phones can record up to 4K60 with all of their cameras, and the main and ultrawide also support 8K30. The Pro video mode unlocks a number of other frame rates, including 24fps across the board and 4K120 for the main and ultrawide cameras. The Samsung-developed pro-grade APV codec is available in addition to the default h.265 (HEVC) and the optional older h.264, and you can record in HDR10+ or Log. Video stabilization is available in all modes on all cameras, and there's also Horizon lock mode.

When it comes to video quality, the ultrawide cameras produce the same results, of course. The main cameras do introduce some differences, with the Ultra having a bit of an advantage at 1x, which gets more substantial at 2x. The Ultra does win by a wide margin at 3x - the vanilla model can compute its way to good 3x stills, but not so much 3x video.

Verdict

At the end of the day, there is no objectively better phone in this comparison. The Galaxy Z Fold8 and the Z Fold8 Ultra share most of their core hardware and deliver broadly comparable display quality, performance, charging, speakers and battery life. The meaningful differences all stem from their very different proportions and what those proportions make each phone better suited for.

The regular Z Fold8 is the more compact, pocket-friendly and arguably more versatile option. Its wider cover screen is excellent for typing and messaging, while both displays make more efficient use of their area for conventional video, books, comics and fixed-aspect games. It also posts slightly better battery life. The Ultra does have the more capable camera system, but the differences are generally small enough that casual shooters should not feel like they are giving up too much by choosing the regular Z Fold8.

The Z Fold8 Ultra, meanwhile, is the better fit for anyone who wants a more conventional phone experience when folded and as much workspace as possible when open. Its larger, nearly square inner display is considerably better suited to productivity, multitasking, tablet layouts and games that scale properly, while its tall cover screen works better for social feeds, long webpages and ultrawide video. It also charges a little faster, performs slightly better in short bursts and consistently captures marginally better photos and videos, with its dedicated telephoto camera giving it the clearest hardware advantage.

So, this one really does come down to form factor. Pick the Galaxy Z Fold8 if its compact shape and content-friendly screens appeal to you more. Pick the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra if you prefer the established book-style foldable design and value maximum workspace. Neither choice is inherently superior; it is simply a matter of which set of compromises suits you better.
