Apple wants to bring Face ID to the iPhone Duo 2, but it's running out of time, according to Bloomberg's Mark Gurman. The second-generation foldable, code-named V78, is planned to launch next fall, which means Apple needs to lock in its hardware features right around now - and that deadline is threatening to undercut the company's ambitions for the device.

Apple iPhone Duo

The current iPhone Duo relies on a Touch ID sensor for biometric authentication, which early reviewers weren't too fond of. Gurman says Apple has two priorities for its successor: bringing back Face ID and adding an Action button. The latter could be done by merging the Action button with the Camera Control button, which would make sense given the limited space inside the device and the fact that many users say they rarely use the camera button anyway.

Face ID is the top priority, though. Apple has already developed iPhone Duo 2 prototypes with it, as Gurman said last month, and engineers are still working to integrate the facial recognition system into the outer display. Given the tight timeline, however, it's a toss-up whether the feature will make it into the final consumer version, and Gurman says he doesn't have a wager either way. If it doesn't make it next year, it should arrive with the model after that.

Other goals for the next couple of generations include adding a telephoto camera and making the foldable thinner and lighter overall. Interestingly, Gurman says customer feedback on the first Duo shouldn't translate into hardware changes until the Duo 3. With the original Duo only just on sale and Apple already needing to finalize its successor, there simply isn't enough time to incorporate buyer survey results, so the Duo 2 will reflect priorities Apple set before the first Duo was even announced.
