Samsung launched the Galaxy A37 in March and released it in April, so the company is now almost wrapping up the development of its successor, the A38, for a plausible early 2027 launch. The device has now been spotted in the Geekbench online database with the model number SM-A386B.

This is the global model, and it's powered by the MediaTek Dimensity 7500 SoC, which in the specific prototype which ran the benchmark was paired with 6GB of RAM. The phone will unsurprisingly run Android 17 when it launches, with One UI 9 on top of course.

As you can see in the screenshot above, the Galaxy A38 managed a single-core score of 928 and a multi-core score of 2,687 in Geekbench 7.1.0 for Android. Interestingly, Samsung used the Exynos 1480 in the A37, so the A38 will mark a switch to MediaTek. Note that the A38 may be offered with multiple RAM amounts, as its predecessor was (it started at 6GB and went all the way up to 12GB).

According to a recent rumor, the Galaxy A38 will have a smaller sensor for its 50MP main camera - the 1/1.95" type Sony IMX882, versus the A37's 1/1.56" type Sony IMX906. We assume the company hopes you won't notice given that the resolution will stay the same, and this is obviously a way to cut some costs in the context of the RAM crisis. The A38 will however have a 20% larger battery than its predecessor at 6,000 mAh, so there's that.
