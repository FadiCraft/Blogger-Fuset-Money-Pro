Apple's iPhone 18 Pro Max and Google's Pixel 11 Pro XL represent two different approaches to the premium smartphone. Apple's latest flagship brings a powerful new chipset and a variable-aperture main camera, while Google counters with a lighter design, a brighter display, and its own take on Android. But the iPhone can cost around 500-600 bucks more at current street prices.

But what are you actually giving up for those savings? Let’s put these two phones head to head and find out.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max has a larger screen and is the bigger and heavier device. It features Ceramic Shield panels and an aluminum chassis, but as far as protection goes, the highlight is the enhanced IP68 rating for water resistance up to 6 meters of depth.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL is 23 grams lighter and features a similar build with Gorilla Glass Victus panels and an aluminum frame. However, its IP68 rating only covers submersion up to 1.5 meters.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL also features HiLight, a set of multicolor LEDs inside the camera flash that glow in different patterns when interacting with Gemini or receiving calls from favorite contacts, with customizable colors for each contact. It's a neat idea, but its functionality is surprisingly limited, making it feel more like a gimmick than a genuinely useful feature.

Winner: Tie.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max features a 6.9-inch LTPO OLED screen with 460ppi density, 120Hz refresh rate support, HDR10 and Dolby Vision support. It has oleophobic and anti-reflective coatings. We measured 969nits for maximum automatic and 2,722nits for peak brightness.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL packs a 6.8-inch LTPO OLED screen with 486ppi density, 120Hz refresh rate, and HDR10+ support. The screen is brighter in our tests – we captured 2,333 nits for maximum auto and 3,118 nits for peak brightness.

Winner: Pixel 11 Pro XL. While Apple’s display offers more features (Dolby Vision, anti-reflective coating), the Pixel 11 Pro XL has a smaller punch-hole, higher pixel density, and higher brightness.

Battery life

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is powered by a 5,391mAh battery for the nanoSIM model (as tested) and an even larger 5,567mAh cell for the eSIM one. The Pixel 11 Pro XL battery has a slightly smaller capacity at 5,115mAh.

While both phones did very well on our battery life test, the Active Use Score of the iPhone 18 Pro Max is simply great, beating the Pixel across all four tests.

Winner: iPhone 18 Pro Max.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max retail box states a maximum wired charging power rating of 54W, up from 42W for the 17 Pro Max. The phone also supports up to 25W wireless charging.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL supports up to 45W fast wired charging and up to 25W fast wireless charging with the magnets built-in, just like the iPhone's MagSafe.

Speaker test

The iPhone 18 Pro Max and the Pixel 11 Pro XL feature hybrid stereo speaker systems, where the earpiece is doubling as a speaker. Both phones scored a Very Good mark in our loudness test. Listening to the samples, we can hear the iPhone offers a richer lower-frequency range, and for this – the sample sounds simply better, more complete.

Winner: Tie in Loudness.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max uses Apple's new 2nm A20 Pro chipset, featuring a 6-core CPU and 7-core GPU, with Apple promising up to 40% faster graphics performance compared to the A19 Pro. The Neural Engine has also doubled to 32 cores. Another significant change is the new side-by-side memory architecture, which places the DRAM next to the processor die. Combined with a wider 96-bit memory bus, Apple claims 50% higher memory bandwidth. All storage variants, ranging from 256GB to 2TB, come with 12GB of RAM.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL relies on the 3nm Tensor G6, with Google promising better efficiency and a 50% increase in AI processing power over the previous generation. Its IMG CXTP-48-1536 GPU is less competitive in graphics performance and gaming compatibility, and lacks hardware ray-tracing support. The Pixel comes with 12GB of RAM in its 256GB version and 16GB in the 512GB and 1TB models.

The iPhone 18 Pro Max has a commanding lead in our benchmarks, scoring about 70% higher in Geekbench 6 multi-core and more than twice as high in 3DMark Wild Life Extreme. These are substantial differences, particularly for demanding games and other intensive workloads.

That said, the Pixel 11 Pro XL still offers more than enough performance for everyday use, and the benchmark gap doesn't necessarily translate into an equally dramatic difference in day-to-day responsiveness.

Winner: iPhone 18 Pro Max. Apple's A20 Pro delivers substantially more CPU and GPU performance, giving it considerably more headroom for demanding applications and games.

Software

The iPhone 18 Pro Max runs iOS 27, while the Pixel 11 Pro XL comes with Android 17. Both offer mature and polished software experiences, though with different approaches to customization, multitasking and integration with their respective ecosystems. The iPhone makes particular sense if you already use other Apple devices, while the Pixel offers deeper integration with Google's services and more flexibility in how you use your phone.

Both manufacturers also put considerable emphasis on AI, with intelligent features integrated throughout their interfaces. Apple Intelligence and Google's Gemini-based features take different approaches, and their usefulness will largely depend on which services you use. One notable difference for European buyers is that Apple's new Siri AI assistant is not yet available on iPhones in the EU, while Gemini is available on the Pixel.

Google guarantees seven years of OS and security updates for the Pixel. Apple doesn't make an equivalent seven-year commitment for the iPhone, but has a long track record of supporting its devices for about 7 years too.

Winner: Tie. At this level, the choice largely comes down to your preferred operating system and ecosystem, though we'd give Google a slight nod for the wider availability of AI features.

Camera comparison

More similar than they are different, the two phones pack triple rear setups with 48MP-50MP sensors all around. The main camera focal lengths are the same, the Pixel's ultrawide is a little wider than the iPhone's already pretty wide ultrawide, and the Pixel's telephoto is a little longer than the iPhone's telephoto, but it's certainly not a world of difference.

Okay, so the iPhone's main camera has a variable aperture lens, but as we established in its own review, that's not of all that much practical use. That aside, the 18 Pro Max's 48MP 1/1.28" sensor is just about the same in sheer size as the 50MP 1/1.3" one on the 11 Pro XL.

The ultrawides both use something like a 1/2.5" sensor and feature AF. The iPhone's lens is listed at 13mm (though it does say 14mm in the EXIF data), while the Pixel is at around 12mm. Both are, indeed, very wide, but the Pixel's is wider, obviously so.

When it comes to the telephotos, the iPhone uses a smallish sensor (1/2.55") with a lens that's listed at 100mm. The Pixel had a sensor of this optical format last year, but the 11 Pro XL was upgraded to a 1/1.95" imager, so that just might give it a bit of an advantage over the 18 Pro Max. The sensor-shift stabilization vs. the lens-based stabilization of the Pixel is more of a curious observation than a practically significant difference.

When it comes to selfies, the approaches are a lot more different, though to be fair, there's not a lot of models with a multi-aspect selfie camera sensor like the iPhone. The iPhone 18 Pro Max, like the previous generation, comes with a square-ish-but-really-octagonal sensor that can take landscape photos (18MP ones) when you're holding the phone vertically (works the other way around too), and it's paired with a wide-angle, 20mm-equivalent lens. The Pixel, on the other hand, uses a conventionally-proportioned sensor with a 42MP nominal resolution (10MP resultant images) and a 17mm lens (going by the company's specified field of view).

The camera samples (both photo and video) have been taken on separate occasions with the two phones.

Image quality

Daylight

A key difference in the out-of-the-box processing of the two phones is that the iPhone will save 24MP files, while the Pixel defaults to 12.5MP, so direct comparisons can be a bit tricky. With that in mind, we'd still be inclined to say that the iPhone just might have a bit of a detail advantage in its default output compared to the Pixel. The Pixel has its Pixel way of rendering detail, its characteristic grittiness giving it a nice well-defined look. We wouldn't necessarily say one is better than the other in terms of dynamic range - both have great contrast and well-developed tonal extremes. Color output is also finely tuned to each brand's specifics, though the iPhone does tend to render things warmer in our experience.

At the 2x zoom level there's no contest - the iPhone's results are substantially sharper than those from the Pixel.

At the telephoto cameras' native zoom levels, the iPhone's 24MP shots are anything but convincing in their 1:1 detail and we'd say the Pixel has a meaningful advantage in its sharpness and definition. Again, there's no real advantage when it comes to dynamic range and in terms of colors it's once again each brand's fairly typical take, and we wouldn't say one is objectively better than the other.



Daylight comparison, telephoto camera: iPhone 18 Pro Max (4x) • Pixel 11 Pro XL (5x)

At twice the telephoto's focal length, the two phones are more or less on par, we reckon. The iPhone does pretty decently, even if its output is a little lacking in microcontrast - there's plenty of detail in the shots even if it's rendered in a somewhat laid-back fashion. The Pixel, in contrast, is a lot more heavy-handed with the sharpening, and there can be some AI-enhanced (read 'made up' detail) that can be jarring at times. We're somehow more fond of the iPhone 8x images than the Pixel's 10x, even though it was the other way around at 4x vs. 5x.

Low light

In the dark, we'd give the nod to the iPhone's main camera at 1x - it tends to leave more texture in relatively low-contrast areas, which the Pixel would rather smooth out, though it's not necessarily a universal or very pronounced advantage. Both do great with dynamic range, but the iPhone just might be a touch more protective of its highlights - not that the Pixel's slightly harsher spotlights are bad.



Low-light comparison, main camera (1x): iPhone 18 Pro Max • Pixel 11 Pro XL

The iPhone would be our pick for 2x zoom level shooting at night, as it was during the day, maintaining better sharpness and detail - the gap gets wider the darker the scene.



Low-light comparison, main camera (2x): iPhone 18 Pro Max • Pixel 11 Pro XL

Comparing the telephotos at 4x vs. 5x, the Pixel's shots are sharper, cleaner, better exposed, just more refined overall, even if its colors can be somewhat off.



Low-light comparison, telephoto camera: iPhone 18 Pro Max (4x) • Pixel 11 Pro XL (5x)

At 8x vs. 10x, we'd also prefer the Pixel's results - that sensor-size advantage does seem to translate into real-world benefits.

The iPhone's photos are noisier, almost blatantly so, to which the Pixel replies with an overall softness, and that's neither here nor there.

Selfies

Selfies differ somewhat substantially in detail rendition, the Pixel showing heavily sharpened faces while the iPhone smoothens skin somewhat but then does some weird pixelation with facial hair - neither is exactly class-leading. The Pixel is particularly bad at night, while the iPhone can be better, if it manages to get its focus right.



Selfies comparison: iPhone 18 Pro Max • Pixel 11 Pro XL

Video quality

In daytime videos the iPhone has the upper hand in sharpness on the ultrawide and it's also better on the main camera at 1x, and more so at 2x. Some of that is due to its sharpening being dialed higher, while the Pixel is a little more relaxed, but ultimately the iPhone has a bit of an advantage. There's a bit more of that on the telephoto end - the iPhone footage at the native zoom level has better definition than the Pixel's and the gap widens at 8x vs. 10x.

Verdict

The iPhone 18 Pro Max is our pick if battery life and performance are your priorities. It lasted longer in all four battery tests, reached 100% a little sooner over USB-C, and led in our CPU and graphics benchmarks.

The Pixel 11 Pro XL is the better fit if you prefer Google's take on Android, want a lighter phone, or value a brighter screen outdoors. Its display was brighter in both of our measurements, and its native 5x telephoto gives you more zoom than the iPhone's 4x lens.
