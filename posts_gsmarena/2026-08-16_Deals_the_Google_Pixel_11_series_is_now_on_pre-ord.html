Google launched its new Pixel 11 series earlier this week – there is a new vanilla, two new Pro and one new foldable model. All that’s missing now is an affordable a-phone.

The massive increase in RAM prices has affected the Pixel series. Google now offers 256GB storage as standard on all 11-series models, which is a good thing, but the Pixel 11 Pro and 11 Pro XL start with 12GB of RAM. If you want 16GB (which is what all Pixel 10 Pro and 10 Pro XL phones had), you will have to get a 512GB or a 1TB model. The vanilla phone only comes with 12GB of RAM, while the foldable always gets 16GB.

The Pixel 11 series is currently on pre-order and will be available from August 20. During that time, Amazon is offering a gift card and, if you send in an old phone, you can get a trade-in bonus on top of the old device’s value. The gift card amount and trade-in bonus vary with the model.

The vanilla Google Pixel 11 is powered by the same Tensor G6 chipset as its more expensive siblings. However, it misses out on the HiLight RGB LED. Oh well. At least it has a larger main camera sensor than the Pixel 10 (48MP 1/1.56” vs. 1/2.0”), while keeping the 5x/112mm 10.8MP telephoto and 13MP ultra-wide cameras the same.

The new model also features faster 25W Pixelsnap magnetic wireless charging (up from 15W), but wired charging is the same at a slow-ish 30W rate. The battery capacity is the same too – Google is betting that the more efficient G6 chip will improve battery life.

The Google Pixel 11 Pro does have HiLight – it’s an RGB LED next to the cameras on the back, which serves as a notification light and is also used by the Gemini AI. The Pro model has a 6.3” OLED display, which is the same size as the vanilla phone, but it is a sharper (1280p+ vs. 1080p+) LTPO panel. The battery capacity is basically the same as last year and so is the 30W wired charging. Wireless charging has been upgraded to 25W like on the vanilla model.

The Pro has more capable cameras, though they are mostly the same as last year. This includes a 50MP main with a large 1/1.3” sensor and a 48MP ultra-wide with an autofocus lens. The telephoto camera is different – it has a 48MP 1/1.95” sensor and a 5x/106mm lens compared to a 48MP 1/2.55” sensor and a 113mm lens on the 10 Pro. It remains to be seen how much of a difference this makes.

The Google Pixel 11 Pro XL is just a larger Pro – it has a 6.8” display (1334p+, LTPO) and a 5,115mAh battery. That’s not so big – in fact, it’s 85mAh smaller than the 10 Pro XL battery. The new XL model has 45W wired and 25W wireless charging, the same as the old XL. Wired charging seems to be marginally faster, though, going up to 75% in half an hour, compared to 70%. We will be testing that in our review.

The new Google Pixel 11 Pro Fold is slimmer and lighter than its predecessor – it measures 10.1mm folded (5.0mm unfolded) and weighs 239g. That’s still thicker and heavier than competing book-style foldables, but it’s a decent improvement over the rather chunky 10 Pro Fold, which was 10.8mm folded (5.2mm unfolded) and weighed 258g.

This slimming down had a negative impact on the battery, which now only has 4,806mAh capacity. This puts it below the Z Fold8 Ultra (5,000mAh) and the Pixel 10 Pro Fold (5,015mAh). At least wireless charging was boosted to 25W, but wired is still only 30W.

Google also equipped the new foldable with the vanilla phone’s 48MP 1/1.56” main camera, which replaces the 48MP 1/2.0” module from last year. That sensor might have been okay for the Pixel 10, but it was certainly not suitable for the pricey Pixel 10 Pro Fold.
