The iPhone has always had a great selection of games, including some AAA titles from older console generations. However, it was with the launch of the iPhone 15 Pro that Apple opened the gates for current-generation games to be ported over to the mobile platform for the first time. Such was the graphical leap of the Apple A17 Pro that, for the first time, we had the latest Resident Evil and Assassin’s Creed titles being available on a pocketable device. To this day, the iPhone 15 Pro remains the minimum requirement for some of these titles.

At the time, we covered many of these releases on the iPhone 15 Pro in dedicated reviews. On one hand, it was genuinely impressive that some of these titles were running on a smartphone at all. On the other hand, it was clear that they were pushing the capabilities of the little device to its absolute limit, and that it wasn’t always able to keep up.

Three years since then, we now have the iPhone 18 Pro. We already have full reviews of both sizes of the device, and can tell you precisely how much more powerful the new hardware is. But it’s one thing to compare benchmark scores, and another to test real-world games to see how far the new device has managed to move the needle, something you can do quite easily with the iPhone’s ever-increasing library of games.

So we decided to revisit some of the games we have looked at in the past to see how far things have come in three years, and how the new device fares with these titles. It would also be an opportunity to see how the old iPhone 15 Pro deals with them three years on.

Resident Evil Village

The first game we looked at back in the day was Resident Evil Village. This would be the first of many Resident Evil releases on the platform, as developer Capcom went on to release Resident Evil 2, 3, 4, and 7 over the next two years.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

Village is unique among these releases, as it remains the only one that offers a full set of graphical settings, something you’ll see is otherwise shockingly rare. At the time, I complained about the complex graphical menus for a mobile release, instead suggesting it should have just shipped with optimized settings. Looking back at it now, it was a dumb thing to say, as the availability of those options now allows the game to take full advantage of the new iPhone 18 Pro, in a way most of the other games here cannot.

For the sake of testing, I set both devices to the Prioritize Graphics preset, which auto-configures a bunch of options. In the opening village area, the iPhone 15 Pro struggled to maintain even 30fps, hovering somewhere around a very cinematic 22-24fps with constant stutters. Meanwhile, the iPhone 18 Pro easily managed over twice that, usually reporting close to 50fps. Turn the settings preset down to Balanced, and while the iPhone 15 Pro can now maintain a steady 30fps with a manual frame lock, the iPhone 18 Pro is bumping against the game’s 60fps ceiling.

The difference is palpable. Village never felt great on the iPhone 15 Pro, as Capcom’s solution to its first mobile port was to just dump the Mac version on it and call it a day. The game especially struggled with the 15 Pro’s limited 8GB of memory, which was also an issue with some other titles. However, three years later, the same game absolutely flies on the iPhone 18 Pro, with a perfectly playable frame rate even with the settings turned up all the way thanks to a combination of faster CPU and GPU as well as the higher 12GB memory.

Resident Evil 4

Resident Evil 4 was the newest RE game at the time and had a more advanced version of Capcom’s RE Engine. This time around, Capcom had learned its lesson, and instead of pushing the Mac version to iOS and calling it a day, the company spent a bit more time configuring the game for the platform, specifically the iPhone 15 Pro that was the only compatible device at the time.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

The result of this is that the game has absolutely no noteworthy graphics settings to speak of. No resolution adjustment, no frame rate settings, and no visual quality options.

On the plus side, the game runs quite well on the iPhone 15 Pro. Surprisingly so, in fact, as even areas I remember being an issue at launch run at a rock-solid 30 fps. Clearly, at some point, the game was polished further to make it run even better.

The downside of this is that there is little for the iPhone 18 Pro to improve upon. With no graphical settings, the resolution, visuals, and the frame rate are identical to the three-year-old model. This is a shame, as the game looks quite soft on the iPhone due to its 300p internal resolution before upscaling, and some things like Leon’s hair look quite jagged and ugly in motion. The only advantages of the 18 Pro are that it doesn’t stutter while entering new areas like the 15 Pro still does in this game, and the load times are noticeably quicker, but aside from that, you aren’t going to see much improvement from upgrading to this model.

Resident Evil 7

Resident Evil 7 is the oldest of the recent Resident Evil titles, and thus is generally the easiest to run on the mobile platform. When I first reviewed it a couple of years ago, it ran reasonably well on the iPhone 15 Pro. However, despite the limited settings on offer, it is possible to get more out of the game on the iPhone 18 Pro.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

The game offers three presets: prioritize performance, balanced, and prioritize graphics. Unfortunately, the latter two are locked at 30fps, and the first is limited to 60fps. The three presets vary in terms of graphical fidelity, and the performance preset also swaps out MetalFX temporal upscaling with the inferior spatial upscaler. The only graphics option you have control over is the output resolution, which decides the internal rendering resolution.

That resolution option comes in clutch, as you can see the difference between the two devices, at least to some extent. The iPhone 15 Pro struggles to maintain 60fps on the performance preset, coming up in the 40s, when the output resolution is set to display native. The iPhone 18 Pro, on the other hand, hits the 60fps ceiling with no effort at all. We can’t really see the scaling here due to the frame rate limit, but it wouldn’t be a surprise that the device could end up in the 70s or 80s if the frame rate was uncapped.

Resident Evil 3

The final RE game we will look at is Resident Evil 3 remake, which is very similar technically to Resident Evil 2 remake, which is why that one is getting skipped. Like most of the other RE titles, this one does not have any meaningful settings to adjust other than output resolution, so that’s all we have to work with.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

Set to the highest native resolution option, the iPhone 15 Pro is not able to maintain the fixed 30fps frame cap in this game in the test scene, coming in at a very cinematic 24fps, with frequent drops below that. Meanwhile, the iPhone 18 Pro can stick to the 30fps limit throughout. Unfortunately, due to the frame cap, we once again cannot see what the true potential of the iPhone 18 Pro hardware is in this game.

Death Stranding

Moving on from the barrage of Resident Evil titles, we have Death Stranding by Kojima Productions, more specifically, the Director’s Cut.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

In terms of customizability, this is the worst title here, as it doesn’t even have a graphics menu. Nothing in this game can be adjusted visually, save for brightness. The game also runs at a rather abysmal 720p output resolution, meaning the internal rendering resolution is a laughable 360p. The graphics settings internally have also been set to close to their lowest values available on the PC or Mac.

You’d think then that the game would just stick to its 30fps frame cap all the time on the iPhone 15 Pro. Well, yes and no. A lot of the time, the game is running at 30fps, especially when you’re just walking, climbing, or running around, which is most of this game. However, the moment you start moving rapidly through the open world on vehicles or zip lines, or get into large enemy battles, the frame rate tanks. The culprit here seems to be either the CPU, the memory, or both.

The iPhone 18 Pro has no such issues. It can maintain that 30fps frame rate all day, every day, regardless of what’s happening on the screen. This is where, once again, I feel like a settings menu would have been an awesome addition, as the iPhone 18 Pro is barely even awake while running this game, and all that processing power could have been put to better use than running a 30fps game with such low visual settings.

Assassin’s Creed Mirage

Mirage is the second-to-last mainline AC entry, and the only game in the series available on iOS. The game has a fixed 30fps frame rate target with three quality presets, with no further adjustability.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

On the iPhone 15 Pro, Mirage’s performance was rather sketchy. Frame rate was often inconsistent, which made playing this game with flowing parkour movement a bit of a chore. There was also an annoying issue with the game, where your character would get suspended in animation as the game loaded data in the background. To my surprise, this issue still exists on the iPhone 15 Pro.

Once again, none of this is a problem on the iPhone 18 Pro. Even on the highest quality preset, the device can maintain the 30fps limit, and at no point did the game do the suspended animation bit. The performance overall was faultless, and since Mirage is a good-looking game on the iPhone otherwise, the gameplay experience on the iPhone 18 Pro overall was rather good.

Hitman World of Assassination

Hitman WOA is a compilation of the last three Hitman games into one. The menus are somewhat convoluted, and the price for the full game is rather absurd, but at least you can purchase the individual missions separately.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

Being a collection of three games spanning several years, the graphical fidelity varies across all missions. For this test, I used the Dubai mission from the newest Hitman 3 to push both devices to their limits. The game does have a free tutorial mission that you can play to assess the gameplay, but that one is by far the easiest to run and doesn’t give a full understanding of how well the game runs.

The Dubai map makes heavy use of the engine’s render-to-texture feature as well as screen space reflections to create the mirror-like reflections across the flooring and glass walls. Render-to-texture essentially renders the room twice and overlays them as a texture, which is why it can render objects out of screen space, similar to ray tracing. The iPhone 15 Pro struggles with a sub-20fps read-out and heavy stuttering at the highest settings, which makes it unplayable unless you drop everything down to the minimum. The iPhone 18 Pro, on the other hand, only had minor stutters on occasion, either due to shader compilation or due to traversal, and otherwise stuck to the max 30fps limit.

This game has a handful of settings to adjust, including the aforementioned reflections, upscaling technique, and its internal rendering resolution. There is also a frame rate option, which offers a choice of 30, 40, and 60fps, but for some odd reason, the game would not go past 30, no matter what option was chosen. It’s odd decisions like these that make it difficult to fully exploit the hardware capabilities of the iPhone 18 Pro.

Control Ultimate Edition

Control is the last game we are looking at today, and is one of the newer ports to Apple’s platforms. Like Hitman, it's also one we haven't reviewed before. Remedy’s third-person action game is based on the company’s homegrown Northlight Engine, and was a technological tour de force when it released back in 2019 on PC and consoles. The game came to be known for its boundary-pushing graphics, specifically its expansive use of ray tracing effects.


iPhone 15 Pro • iPhone 18 Pro

The game offers the full graphical menu that it does on the PC and the Mac, with only the resolution menu using weird megapixel values instead of proper vertical and horizontal pixel numbers. The game also has the full set of ray tracing options available on supported devices. The frame rate is limited to 30fps, and the only way to increase it is by using frame generation, which doubles it to 60fps.

On the iPhone 15 Pro, the game runs at more or less the lowest quality settings, which still look great in this game. The device can surprisingly maintain 30fps for a lot of the playthrough, with only occasional drops here and there. There isn’t ray tracing support on this device, so those options are unavailable. You can bump up the graphical settings or the resolution, but it’s not recommended, as performance drops below 30 almost instantly. The game speed also seems to be tied to frame rate below this value, as the game itself moves slower when the frame rate drops below 30. For that reason, frame generation is not recommended, as it glitches out and looks weird whenever the frame rate drops.

The iPhone 18 Pro can run the game more or less locked to 30fps even at the highest quality settings. This was incredible to see, as even previous-generation consoles were unable to achieve this feat. Moreover, the 18 Pro also has access to the full suite of ray tracing options. The fact that ray tracing is even possible on a smartphone is still mind-blowing, when you consider not just its GPU but also CPU and memory cost.

Understandably, the iPhone 18 Pro can no longer maintain 30fps with ray tracing settings maxed out. However, if you set the device to medium for standard and ray tracing options, you can still get great image quality with some ray tracing options and more or less locked 30fps. This is truly incredible, and something you cannot achieve on something like a Steam Deck or even the Xbox Series S.

With ray tracing disabled and both devices set to the highest rasterized settings preset with default resolution and upscaling settings, you can see a clear difference between the two iPhones. The iPhone 18 Pro bumps up against the 30fps limit, whereas the iPhone 15 Pro can’t even maintain 15fps, with unplayable performance overall.

Conclusion

In doing these comparisons, we ended up with more than just the obvious takeaway of the newer device being faster. The point was to see how gaming on the iPhone has progressed over the three years since the iPhone 15 Pro, and how that device, as well as the games released on it, have held up over time, especially in light of newer devices.

First, the iPhone 15 Pro was held back by more than just GPU performance. As I found out after testing several games over the years, the biggest bottleneck for gaming performance on the iPhone 15 Pro was actually the 8GB of memory. Most of these games were designed for a lot more than that on their launch platforms, and that was always going to be a difficult problem to overcome. Almost every game here would overwhelm that 8GB memory buffer, causing constant swapping that heavily affected performance and loading times. In fact, I couldn't even capture the screen for this article on the iPhone 15 Pro, because recording video in the background made the games stutter even more.

I’m fairly confident you can make a big difference in that phone’s gaming performance simply by giving it more memory. The iPhone 18 Pro has that extra memory, as well as the extra CPU and GPU horsepower, which is why it is able to push so much further than its three-year-old predecessor. And it can do that without breaking a sweat, as the device often feels barely warm compared to the overheating iPhone 15 Pro, thanks to its much beefier thermal design and more efficient chips.

The games themselves haven’t evolved much. This was the most disappointing aspect of revisiting these games. Many of them have locked graphical settings, which prevent you from taking full advantage of newer devices. Some titles run very similarly across these two phones, when in fact they could look and run so much better on the 18 Pro. This is something that the developers needed to address with updates, but unfortunately a lot of these games have mostly been abandoned since release.

The game releases have also slowed down. Most of the games on this list are Resident Evil titles. Capcom, for its part, truly committed to delivering its titles on the platform. But we haven’t heard any news of its latest Resident Evil Requiem being released on Apple’s platforms. We have Death Stranding on this list, but there’s no news of Death Stranding 2 releasing on the iPhone. The same can be said for sequels to Control and Assassin’s Creed Mirage. As it turned out, the games they did release did not sell well, so you can’t really blame them for not rushing to release more titles.

The gameplay experience isn’t always fun. Don’t get me wrong, I love that you could play these console-class games on a smartphone. But outside of being impressive tech demos, I didn’t feel a strong urge to play them. These are games built to be played on a big screen from your couch, and they lose much of their grandeur when played on a six-inch display. The on-screen controls are irritating, and the UI is often unusably small. You are expected to use a controller, which is an added cost, and after all that, you will be draining the battery of your smartphone, which is hardly ideal. This isn't an issue with the iPhones specifically; it's just the nature of playing console games on a pocketable touchscreen device.

Three years from the launch of the iPhone 15 Pro and Apple’s big push for console-quality gaming, the situation has reached an interesting stage. We now have hardware that can fully realize the vision of developers without the limitations of its predecessor. But developer interest in these big AAA releases seems to have waned, with most realizing the money and consumer interest is in the free-to-play mediocrity with a million ads and gacha systems.

Hopefully, that is not the case, and Apple can use its influence to push more developers to release their big-budget titles on its platforms. They may not always make the most sense, but I love that they exist and push the platform in a way few others can.
