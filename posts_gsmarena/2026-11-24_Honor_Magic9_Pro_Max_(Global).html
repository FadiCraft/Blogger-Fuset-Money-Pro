Introduction

The Honor Magic9 Pro Max is shaping up to be one of the more ambitious camera flagships of the year. The dual 200MP camera system is impressive enough on paper, but the real headline is Honor's collaboration with ARRI, bringing elements of the renowned cinema camera maker's image science and professional video workflow to a smartphone. It's an intriguing proposition, and one we're particularly interested in putting to the test.

There's plenty more to the Magic9 Pro Max than its cameras, though. Honor has completely revamped the design, introducing a new ARRI-inspired camera housing, an innovative antenna arrangement, tougher materials and fresh color options.

The Magic9 Pro Max features a 6.8-inch LTPO OLED display with a 1,320p resolution, 10-bit color depth, a 120Hz refresh rate and Dolby Vision support. Honor also advertises a peak brightness of 10,000 nits under specific conditions, along with an anti-reflective coating.

Under the hood is Qualcomm's most powerful chipset yet, the Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6.

But the cameras are where things get particularly interesting. Both the 200MP main camera and the 200MP telephoto feature optical stabilization with a CIPA 8.0 rating, while the 50MP ultrawide has autofocus and doubles as a close-up shooter. On the front, there's an unusual 55MP square-format selfie camera, paired with a 3D ToF sensor for secure face recognition.

The collaboration with ARRI adds a different dimension to the camera experience, particularly for video creators. The phone supports recording in the ARRI LogC3 curve and ARRI Wide Gamut 3 color space, along with ARRI Looks for previewing different color treatments. Honor has also integrated selected elements of ARRI Image Science into its imaging pipeline, although the extent of these capabilities depends on the shooting mode.

Honor is taking the camera ambitions further with optional accessories, including a teleconverter kit offering a 500mm-equivalent focal length and a SmallRig camera cage with mounting points for grips, LED lights and microphones.

Rounding out the package is a massive battery, rated at 8,000mAh in the version described here, with support for 100W wired and 80W wireless charging. The Chinese model gets an even larger 8,800mAh battery.

Compared to the Magic8 Pro, the Magic9 Pro Max brings upgrades to the display, chipset and battery, but the biggest changes are clearly in the camera department. Between the dual 200MP setup, the ARRI collaboration and the optional shooting accessories, Honor is making a serious push toward mobile filmmaking. Whether those ambitions translate into a better camera experience is one of the main questions we'll be exploring in this review.

Unboxing the Honor Magic9 Pro Max

Our Honor Magic9 Pro Max arrived in a substantial box containing a 100W charger with a USB-A port, a 6A-rated USB cable and a transparent silicone case.

Retail packaging may vary by market, and we suspect that the European version will ship without the charger.
