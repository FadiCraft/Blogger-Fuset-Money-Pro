Apple launched the iPhone Duo last week and unlike most of its releases, it hasn’t been smooth sailing. So, let’s look at the non-folding iPhones instead, plus the two new releases from this week – the theme this week was “OLED”.

We’ll start with a nice middle ground – the Apple iPhone 17. The vanilla 18 isn’t coming out for a few more months, so this is the current offering. We rarely see discounts on Apple products, but this vanilla model is €180 off for the base 256GB variant. The upgrade to 512GB storage is crazy expensive, though.

The Apple iPhone Air is from the same generation as the 17. It has a larger 6.5” display (vs. 6.3”), but in some ways it is smaller – it’s thinner (5.6mm vs. 8mm) and lighter (165g vs. 177g). It’s around 3mm wider, though (74.7mm vs. 71.5mm), so it’s up to you to decide whether you want more screen size or easier one-handed use.

With a tiny 3,149mAh battery, the Air doesn’t last as long as the vanilla phone – 12:44h vs. 14:59h Active Use Score. It uses a version of the Apple A19 Pro chipset that gives it slightly more performance in some tests. Note that the Air has only one camera, a 48MP 1/1.56” wide module, while the vanilla iPhone 17 also has a 48MP 120° ultra-wide. The Air costs less than the vanilla model, especially if you need more storage, so it’s worth comparing the two.

Moving on, Apple refreshed the iPad mini. The previous mini came two years ago. The new model is the first mini with an OLED display and, even better, there is a nano-texture glass option for the 8.5” panel (up from 8.3” for the old LCD model). This does cost extra, though. Early Geekbench results suggest that the new Apple A20 Pro chipset with 12GB of RAM tops the iPad Pros in the single-threaded test (by 8%), but having fewer cores means that the Pros still win the multi-threaded test (by a whopping 53%). But the Pros have M5 chips – the M4-powered Air tablets lose on both the single- and multi-threaded tests.

You can still buy the old iPad mini (2024). It’s cheaper, though we’re not sure that the savings are worth it. The new model is slimmer (5.4mm vs. 6.3mm) and lighter (255g vs. 293g), while losing only a bit of battery capacity (4,900mAh vs. 5,078mAh). We expect that the chipset will easily make up the difference – the A20 Pro is a 2nm part, while the 2024 mini uses a 3nm A17 Pro. Note that the OLED display on the new mini still runs at 60Hz, so you’re not getting a high refresh rate either way. One thing you do lose if you go with the old model is that the 2026 mini is rated IP67, while the 2024 mini lacks an official water resistance rating.

The other new release this week is the MacBook Pro with PROLED XDR display – this is not only the first OLED display on a MacBook (a 120Hz Tandem OLED at that), it’s also the first touch-enabled display on a MacBook. The notch is gone, replaced by a Dynamic Island – there is no Face ID hiding inside, it’s only a camera. Impressively, these M5 Pro and M5 Max powered laptops are 200-300g lighter than non-touch MacBook Pros, depending on the exact models you are comparing. They are also quite a bit more expensive too.
